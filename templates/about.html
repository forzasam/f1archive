About the archive

Formula One history,
made easier to explore.

F1 Archive is an independent project dedicated to preserving and presenting the history of Formula One in a modern, accessible way.

More than a results database

F1 Archive brings together historical championship data, interactive visualisations and original editorial content to tell the story of Formula One seasons, rather than simply presenting a collection of results.

Championship progression charts show how title fights developed from race to race, while race pages, circuit visualisations and contextual features help explain the individual weekends behind the numbers. Season and race stories connect those moments into a wider narrative.

The aim is to make it equally easy to revisit a championship you remember, understand a famous season for the first time, or discover a part of Formula One history you may never have seen.

Built as a lasting archive

Every completed Formula One World Championship season from 1950 onwards is stored locally. This makes the historical archive fast and resilient while reducing its dependence on live third-party services.

The current season is maintained separately through a live archive that is refreshed regularly, while preserving the most recent successful snapshot if its data source is temporarily unavailable. Historical data can also be refreshed when source records are corrected or improved.

About the person behind it

F1 Archive was conceived, designed and developed by Sam Clark, a physics graduate from the University of Warwick and a lifelong Formula One fan. Sam continues to develop and maintain the site's software, data systems and interactive features while overseeing the direction of the archive.

The project grew from the intersection of several interests: software development, data analysis and visualisation, Formula One history, and the challenge of turning a large historical dataset into something intuitive to explore.

What began as an independent development project has grown into an evolving archive combining technical development with editorial work. Contributors can be individually credited for their writing through dedicated author pages, while the underlying platform and its features continue to be developed and expanded.

Where the archive is going

F1 Archive is an ongoing project. New season and race stories, richer historical context and new ways of visualising races and championships will continue to be added as the archive develops.

The long-term goal is simple: make decades of Formula One history feel explorable rather than static.
